Your p99 is not what you think

card page in an article

A stand-in for a blog post, to check that a tool can sit in the middle of prose.

Suppose five hosts each report a p99 latency, and a dashboard shows you the average of those five numbers. That average is not the p99 of anything. It is not the p99 of the fleet, it is not the p99 of any host, and it does not correspond to a request that any user made.

The reason is that a percentile is a position in a sorted list, and positions do not add. Paste your own measurements below and compare the two rows: the true p99 of everything pooled, and the average of the chunks' p99s that a dashboard would have shown you.

Source: tools/percentiles/

The gap widens as the distribution skews, which is precisely when you are looking at the number. A long tail on one host is diluted by four quiet ones.

The same thing happens across time as well as across hosts. A p99 per minute, averaged over an hour, is the same error with a different axis — and it hides exactly the minute you were paged for.
